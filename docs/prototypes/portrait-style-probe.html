Portrait Style Probe

Four candidate directions for resident portraits, all drawn from the same seeded genome so you can compare the same face across treatments. Everything here is generated in code - no image files. This is a shape-and-treatment probe, not final art: the point is to judge legibility, silhouette, and how the styles survive 48px next to 240px.

One Resident, Four Treatments

On A Character Card

Inheritance - Three Generations

Continuous genes (jaw width, eye spacing, nose length, skin value) blend toward the midparent with a bell-shaped deviation. Categorical genes (head shape, hair style, hair colour, eye shape) are diploid: each person carries two alleles and passes one at random, so a grandparent's trait can sit hidden in a parent and resurface in a grandchild. Carried-but-not-expressed alleles are listed under each face. Accessories, shirt and tilt are personal, never inherited.

Contact Sheet - 48 Residents At 64px

The repetition test. Scan for twins. If two faces read as the same person at this size the part library is too small or the silhouette layer is not doing enough work.

Silhouette Test At 48px

Flat black on card background, interior detail removed. If these are not distinguishable from each other, no amount of interior linework will save the small size.

Colour Lineage

Skin, hair and shirt each carry a continuous RGB gene that blends midparent-style, then snaps to the nearest palette entry at render time. A child of a red parent and a blue parent comes out purple - quantized, but recognisably descended from both. Hair additionally rejects any entry within 48 luminance of the skin it sits on, which is what stops pale-on-pale reading as a bald head. Switch Palette to "Mac OS 256" to see it on the portraits themselves.

The Palette

The classic Mac OS 8-bit system palette: a 6x6x6 colour cube on the levels 255/204/153/102/51/0, then 10-step pure red, green, blue and grey ramps on the multiples of 17 the cube skips. 216 + 40 = 256. Hover a swatch for its index and hex.

The Axis Alone

One mouth path, interpolated from a thin line to full lips. These are not two shapes: the line is the lip shape with both contours collapsed onto each other, so the two share an identical command sequence and only the numbers differ. That is the whole requirement for interpolation, and it is the constraint a Figma file has to satisfy on export.

On A Face

Same genome, same everything else, only the lip weight moving.

Combined With Proportion

The lip axis crossed with the proportion cues that carry a masculine or feminine read (jaw width, brow weight, brow height). The axes are independent: a heavy jaw with full lips and a narrow jaw with a line mouth are both reachable, which is what stops the morph from smuggling in a gender assumption.
